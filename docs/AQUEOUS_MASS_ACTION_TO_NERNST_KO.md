# Aqueous mass-action activities to crevice Nernst history

`integration.aqueous_mass_action_to_crevice_nernst_history` takes dimensionless species activities from either a computed, digest-pinned `integration.crevice_composition_to_aqueous_mass_action_history` result or an `integration.aqueous_bdot_speciation_history` result and applies a caller-declared redox quotient at the same exact time and temperature knots. It reuses the existing Nernst calculator rather than creating a second formula implementation.

The caller defines reaction ID, signed quotient stoichiometry, electron count, standard-potential reference, E0 history, provider revision/configuration/record digests and applicability reviews. Every quotient species must exist with a finite positive dimensionless activity in every solved knot. The output retains input, full-result and knot-result digests from the upstream aqueous calculation, as well as the composition-history digest and common asset/location/clock.

The activities use the upstream solver's dimensionless 1 mol/L standard state. A B-dot history keeps every knot's temperature-specific coefficient record in its own upstream input/result digest. Output names the lineage as an aqueous activity history and does not report that digest as a raw composition inventory. The standard potential is not inferred or temperature-adjusted; the caller must provide and review an exact-knot E0 value. The result is a sequential composition/speciation → Nernst handoff. It does not iterate current back into the same composition, infer a reaction, add phases or transport, calculate current or corrosion rate, or convert dissolved metal into wall loss.

Python registry ID: `integration.aqueous_mass_action_to_crevice_nernst_history`; request contract: `AqueousMassActionToNernstHistory`. The composition-history synthetic flow appears in `examples/crevice_electrochemical_composition.py`; `examples/aqueous_bdot_to_nernst_history.py` now carries one B-dot activity history through two Nernst branches into the shared mixed-potential API. Both examples remain unexecuted and are not validation evidence.
